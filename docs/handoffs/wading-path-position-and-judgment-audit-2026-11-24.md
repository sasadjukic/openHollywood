## What would justify a stop short?

The scene-four draft gives the pair a sequence of actions. They reach the eastern wall, scramble into the service plenum and tumble inside (excerpts 0063–0065). They gasp and look deeper (0066–0067). “Further down the shaft” locates an **emergency light**. At the end of the narrow passage “stood the heavy steel housing” (0068). The final sentence identifies **the housing** as the barrier (0069). None gives the pair’s final distance from the housing or their posture there. The earlier distance to the eastern wall belongs to an instant before they cross the room. The plan assigns reaching the housing and standing before the barrier. Those assignments supply the criteria for fulfillment. [Version-bound draft spans and plan fields](../../data/diagnostics/wading-path-be-regression-2026-11-24/capture/run/gold.json)

The sequence makes a reader’s impression of separation plausible. The last completed movement is entry. The narration then follows the pair’s gaze and describes the destination. Q010 B says the claimed position “misstates the depicted endpoint,” and Q010 E says it “conflicts with the depicted staging.” Both answers correctly identify the subject of “stood.” The unsupported step is treating the direction of a gaze and the location of an object as a final observation placing both people elsewhere. **Only Q011 E explicitly says the draft “depicts an earlier stopping point.”** A direct placement of both people a fixed distance short at the cut would settle that question. The Wading Path supplies no such placement. Earlier authored gap controls demonstrate what that evidence looks like.
