## Why the four open-event responses diverged

The claim is that Elias and Maya reached the breaker housing **at some time during scene four**. All four arms cited E1420 on draft version `2f9e80a0-29b4-579f-8075-b6e064788b24`. The draft depicts entry into the plenum and a sightline to the housing (offsets 3995–4804). It first locates the pair three strides short when the light steadies (4806–4945). An earlier visit followed by a return is consistent with these observations. The ordinary draft supplies no affirmative account of that visit and no minimum distance covering the whole scene. The two authored retrospectives establish those stronger conclusions on their respective versions. ## What this answers about the critic

**Harness or model?** All 48 terminals succeeded, every generation matched its pre-count and requested model, and within each case the replay bound all four answers to the same claim and immutable draft. Within a presentation the only request difference between models was the model ID. X added one field asking about the interval covered by the observation. It supplied no answer. A missing draft, truncated delivery, swapped citation or scoring join does not explain these results. Both X arms recovered the narration evidence point when the presentation changed. Only Astra X applied that distinction to the open event. This is evidence that both task framing and the inference in the delivered answer can matter. A single observation per cell cannot isolate the underlying cause or rule out run-to-run variation. [Request parity and scoring](wading-path-sol-astra-scope-freeze-2026-11-25.md) · [48-slot inspection](../../data/diagnostics/wading-path-sol-astra-scope-results-2026-11-25/slot-inspection.json)

**How common and how consequential?** Nineteen of 24 assessments passed both axes. Sixteen of those passes were the same four determinate controls across four arms. On the eight deliberately difficult stop-only assessments, three passed both axes. These deliberately selected cases leave the ordinary critic’s accuracy and the prevalence of this error in manuscripts unmeasured. The immediate effect was limited to a read-only assessment. A false scene-wide denial could matter if a writer were told to add an arrival or remove an intentional ambiguity. No writer ran here, and we observed no edit, rejection of a draft or reader-quality loss. Keep an unsupported denial out of automatic revision authority. Human judgment about whether the ambiguity *works* belongs to the existing story-quality evaluation. ## Proposed repeat on a fresh scene family

Use an authored **planetarium rehearsal** in scene four. The focal event is the projection of a blue comet onto the dome. This changes the people, setting, action and vocabulary from a pair approaching a destination. In every version the dome carries no image when the house lights come up and stays blank through the cut.

1. **Admit the sources locally.** Prepare a complete scene, plan and review context. Vary only the history that precedes or is recounted at the shared closing observation. In the open version, leave an earlier projection possible without recounting it. In the exclusion version, explicitly cover the entire rehearsal with a witness ruling it out. In the confirmation version, recount the projection and its extinction before the lights come up. Ensure the plan or another field never states what actually happened in the open draft.
2. **Freeze six claims before seeing output.** Ask both whether the draft explicitly recounts the projection and whether it occurred at any time in scene four. Gold for narration/event is unsupported/uncertain on the open history, unsupported/unsupported on the exclusion, and supported/supported on the confirmation. Pin source versions, full context, decisive spans and separate one-point judgment and evidence rules. Accept equivalent evidence. A dark dome at the cut alone earns zero evidence on every scene-wide claim. Rehearse one correct open answer and both overreads locally.
3. **Repeat the four matched arms.** Sol 6.2 and Astra 6.2 at high reasoning, each with B and the exact existing X wording for the claim type. Keep input comparable within each matched set and counterbalance all arm pairs. The proposed ceiling is **24 serial input counts before up to 24 serial generations, zero retries**, with one observation per case and arm. Keep the prior per-generation caps of 160,000 input and 4,096 output tokens. Recheck the cost ceiling at freeze time. Stop globally at the first failed count, binding, delivery, usage, budget or deadline check. A valid semantic miss is scored. Submit the exact requests, costs and journal for separate approval.
4. **Decide on the open event and controls separately.** Score evidence with model, presentation, label and order masked, then seal and join the labels. A second-family Astra-X-only recovery requires 1/1 on the open event, 1/1 on its narration counterpart and all four controls, with each of the three contemporaneous comparison arms missing at least one open-event point. If Sol B also passes the open event, report baseline recovery and no unique need for Astra X. If Sol X matches Astra X, a frontier advantage has not transferred on that case. If Astra B also succeeds, the new result cannot establish that explicit scope was necessary for Astra. An Astra X miss or control regression holds escalation. Any missing cell makes the comparison inconclusive. Report both scores, costs and time for all arms. A second-family recovery would justify testing a selective second opinion. Writer action would still need separate qualification.

This is a proposal for a fresh authored control family. Its requests have not been frozen or dispatched. B remains the default, live calls are paused and the full workflow canary is on hold.
