## The no-match obligation

The current source reads: **“No handwriting match is established by the end of
this scene.”** The draft explicitly establishes a beyond-doubt match; its ending
adds dread but does not retract or disprove that result. All efforts correctly
recognize the actual PC endpoint violation. Max's additional reversal exception
occurs in its explanation of the obligation, not in a missed judgment about the
actual unretracted event.

The saved max explanation is retained as a semantic failure under the frozen
contract: it adds an exception that the assignment does not authorize. No source
or score is rewritten. The earlier report's sensitivity observation also remains:
even waiving that penalty would not make max pass its frozen continuation screen.

The production source audit gives a more precise limit on that conclusion:

- `ScenePlan.exit_state` is a nonempty text field. The assignment contract projects
  it unchanged, alongside outcome and other exact assignment fields.
- The shared boundary policy distinguishes a decision, an attempt and an established
  result; it permits inconclusive tests and incidental follow-through when they do
  not establish an unassigned result. A broader goal or a more specific future
  proof does not override the current endpoint.
- These contracts do **not** assign every natural-language obligation a typed
  event-history or closing-belief interpretation. The read-only assessment result
  also leaves support and edit necessity unassessed.

Consequently, the supported conclusion is local: **this frozen condition licenses
no such reversal exception**. It is not a general rule that characters cannot
change their minds, that an apparent result can never be disproved, or that the
word “by” alone supplies a deterministic temporal type.

Six authored hypothetical traces check the distinction without parsing prose:

| Declared facts | Explicit prohibition on establishing a match by the deadline | Explicit requirement that the character not believe in a match at closing |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Purposeful decision and inconclusive test; no definitive result or closing belief | Met | Met |
| Definitive match persists at closing | Unmet | Unmet |
| Definitive result is retained in event history, but the character withdraws belief | Unmet | Met |
| Later reliable disproof leaves what was truly established unresolved | Unassessed | Met when closing belief is explicitly absent |
| No definitive result; closing belief unspecified | Met | Unassessed |
| No decision and no match | No-match condition met; the separate decision remains missing | Met when closing belief is absent |

These are **authored contrast fixtures, not observed model outcomes**. They evaluate
two expressly declared hypothetical contracts over supplied facts. The original
untyped exit-state text always remains marked as requiring contextual
interpretation; no regex, keyword or model statement selects a contract. A later
reliable disproof must not be silently equated with ordinary doubt, and an earlier
false belief must not automatically be treated as an established narrative fact.

If a future production change makes obligation scope explicit, it should bind that
scope to the authoritative assignment/version. It should distinguish event history,
character knowledge/belief and the closing state, preserve legitimate provisional
tests, and require explicit authority for exceptions. This is a design finding only;
no schema, prompt, parser or acceptance rule is changed here.
