# What explicit scope changed in the Sol and Astra assessments

**Explicit scope improved some delivered explanations. Its effect on the critic’s factual decisions is still uncertain.** Across The Wading Path and the planetarium, X improved six evidence scores and one judgment relative to the same model using B. The strongest repeated finding was that both models explained the boundary between narration and occurrence better on both families’ open narration claims. The strongest individual result was Astra X correctly leaving the earlier Wading Path visit open. That decision has one Astra X observation on that draft. ## What we can infer about chance

The recurring improvement on the two open narration claims gives a concrete reason to keep testing X. In four of four model-by-family comparisons the added instruction elicited a better-scoped explanation under the frozen rubric. The changed wording in the answers is visible, and the request comparison isolates the instruction within each pair. The improvement was more consequential on The Wading Path, where it removed a false inference, than on the planetarium, where it made an accurate answer easier to check.

**Sol X** earned three additional evidence points and corrected zero labels across 12 cases. **Astra X** also earned three additional evidence points and corrected one label. With one generation per case, model and presentation in each comparison, we cannot estimate how often either result would recur on the same request. The two models also share the same drafts and gold, and each family’s narration and event questions share passages. Treating the 24 pairs as independent trials would overstate confidence. The planetarium packet had about 2,400–2,500 input tokens and The Wading Path about 91,000. Setting, task difficulty and context length changed together. The evidence supports a targeted benefit in explanations and leaves the reliability of frontier escalation open. These read-only assessments measured neither autonomous writer behavior nor human story preference. ## Next steps

1. **Check grading materiality locally first.** Give an independent reader the 16 saved explanations for Wading Path SN/SE and planetarium ON/PE with model, presentation, returned label, order and prior grades masked. Supply the exact drafts, claims and the frozen criteria. For each answer, record whether its evidence supports the conclusion without overreach and, separately, whether it supplies every diagnostic detail. Seal the readings before joining the old scores. A disagreement about the unreported middle cues or the image going out can be settled without another generation.
2. **If the frontier-routing question still matters, test repeatability on the hard request.** Propose four fresh observations per arm on the *same* Wading Path SE request, keeping both models at high and counterbalancing the four arm orders. Give each arm one whole-scene exclusion and one confirmed earlier visit as controls. That is **24 counts before up to 24 generations, zero retries**. Count only fresh trials in the decision. Before seeking approval, freeze separate judgment and evidence rules, both controls, costs and stops. A provisional threshold for further testing is at least 3/4 grounded SE passes for Astra X, at least two more than each of the other three arms, and both controls passing. Sol B matching the result would remove the observed need for escalation. Report ties, losses and any false denials even if totals agree. A missing cell leaves the comparison inconclusive. A successful same-input repeat would warrant a held-out, production-length family before any routing change.
3. **Keep B as the production default.** X remains a candidate for read-only assessment of claims where the time span of the evidence matters. Keep ungrounded denials away from automatic revision. Story-quality work can proceed independently through the existing human review of feel, flow, character and story-breaking details.
