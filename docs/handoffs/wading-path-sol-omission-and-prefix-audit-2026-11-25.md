# The Wading Path · Why the two Sol reviewers ruled out an earlier visit

**Both models found the right passage. Their explanations turned a fact about the narrated approach into a conclusion about the whole scene.** All twelve responses cited the correct version of `E1420`. Each model passed five of six judgments, four of six evidence checks and four cases on both axes. Replay reproduced all 24 original slots and the sealed grades. ## What the paired answers establish

| History | Explicit narration · judgment/evidence for each model | Visit during the scene · judgment/evidence for each model |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Measured stop alone | 1/0. Both correctly rejected explicit narration, then asserted the visit did not happen. | 0/0. Both returned `unsupported` against `uncertain` gold. |
| Earlier visit explicitly excluded | 1/1. Both described what was recounted and included the retrospective. | 1/1. Both identified the minimum distance covering the entire scene. |
| Earlier visit explicitly confirmed | 1/1. Both located the narrator's explicit account of joint arrival. | 1/1. Both located the earlier visit and the return up the passage. |

On the measured-stop draft, the pair reach the eastern wall, enter the plenum and see the housing farther down the passage (3995–4804). They stop three strides short and stay there (4806–4945). “Stayed there” carries their position forward from the stop. A reader can verify the absence of an explicit arrival by inspecting the entire account. To rule out a physical visit at *any time* takes evidence covering the earlier interval as well.

The two event explanations fail in slightly different ways. Sol 6.1 expressly inferred “no earlier arrival followed by retreat.” Sol 6.2 accurately summarized the approach and ended with “they reach the wall, not the housing.” Its conclusion and returned label leave no allowance for a visit before that final approach. On the narration claim, both models went beyond identifying the omission and asserted nonarrival during the scene. The evidence grades were assessed independently of those labels. [Verbatim answers and adjudications](../../tests/fixtures/wading_path_sol_versions_observed.json)

The paired controls show exactly what would change that conclusion. Every version has the same first 4,945 characters. The negative retrospective (4947–5149) gives both people a minimum distance from the opening huddle through the cut. The positive retrospective (4947–5132) explicitly places them together at the housing after plenum entry and before the light steadies, then recounts their return. Both versions can therefore end with the same three-stride gap. Neither retrospective is evidence about what happened in the unamended draft. [Three frozen histories and gold](../../tests/fixtures/wading_path_narration_event_gold.json)

**A testable application-level explanation.** In all twelve requests, the claim and evidence predicates both say `observed_event`, the unresolved-qualifier list is empty, and the brief gives the same general instruction about the claim's interval or instant. The text of the active claim does distinguish explicit narration from occurrence. The structured declaration gives the assessor no corresponding distinction between a complete account of what was written and exhaustive evidence of the characters’ movements. The fluent, apparently continuous approach makes a first visit seem unlikely; the shared declaration is a plausible contributor to treating that reading as a contradiction. The saved answers show the overreach. This experiment did not vary the declaration and cannot establish its causal effect or either model’s internal reasoning. ## OpenAI prefix guidance

**The diagnostic had no earlier reasoning to preserve on input.** Each generation was a fresh assessment with one system message and one user message. Its count request carried the same model, messages, schema and reasoning setting. The count produced a token count, with no reasoning history for generation. The model comparison started each arm independently. Chaining answers between arms or between claims would give later assessments information their peers never saw. All twelve generation receipts explicitly reported `input_transformations: []`. No captured evidence links a dropped reasoning item to the failure. The recommendations above depend on the kind of call. OpenAI documents the handling of instruction changes, tool restrictions, compaction and cache diagnostics on the linked page. Its reasoning guide describes carrying ordered input and output items, including opaque reasoning, when continuing a stateless call on supported models. **One measurable opportunity for independent calls is cache layout.** The system message has 2,595 characters. Within each history, switching from narration to event changes the serialized user message after just 801 shared characters, in the claim description ahead of the large native story context. The user messages contain over 343,000 characters. These are local string measurements; they do not identify the server’s cache boundary or explain the zero reads. If cache efficiency becomes a priority, first render an offline variant with reusable developer instructions and immutable shared story material first, followed by the active claim and variable brief. Verify source/version authority, schema, scope and model-pair parity before proposing any provider test. Changing message role or layout would itself be an experimental change. **Production scope.** The inspected blueprint and production executors assemble fresh messages for each operation, including retry or repair context where applicable. The shared OpenAI adapter sends `store: false` and projects message roles and text into Responses input. Its production response mapper skips reasoning items, in keeping with ADR 0040. A future same-specialist follow-up that genuinely needs reasoning continuity would need a separately designed provider-local, ephemeral handoff: retain the ordered inputs and returned items in memory and append the new task input. Keep opaque items out of story artifacts, logs and durable domain state. Restart from the pinned artifacts when that ephemeral state is unavailable. This is conditional design work; the captured one-shot comparison gives no reason to share reasoning across independent reviews. ## Recommended next step

**Rehearse an evidence-coverage check locally before another model comparison.** Use the same six gold cases and both sets of saved explanations. Give each claim an explicit reading: what the selected text recounts, or whether an event occurred during a specified interval. For each negative event conclusion, require the assessor to locate the evidence that rules out a visit over that interval. For a negative narration conclusion, check whether the reason stays within what the complete text recounts. Keep the supplied label and the evidence assessment separate. A conclusion with insufficient coverage can be held for review without turning an ambiguous scene into a writer edit.

Use the frozen answers as acceptance controls. The check must flag both stop-only event rejections and both overbroad narration explanations, retain all eight retrospective passes and retain the earlier S2 endpoint pass under its narrower claim. Include a valid paraphrase and a reason citing only the final gap to test whether the check distinguishes coverage from word matching. Freeze the expected outcomes before writing the check. This proposal changes neither the prose nor the provider presentation and requires zero inference calls.
