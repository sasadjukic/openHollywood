## What this changes in the diagnosis

The user review does not support presenting the small wording and preparation
changes as demonstrated literary harm. It supports treating this scene as a promising
foundation whose local variations did not materially change this reader's experience.
Future tests should measure whether an edit changes understanding, emotional impact,
character, flow, or a binding story obligation, rather than elevate difference size
into a quality objective.

The distinction between mechanical completion and perceived quality remains useful.
The reader recorded literary ties even on pairs whose outside-endpoint compliance
differs. Optional duplication can remain without becoming a story-breaking concern
for this reader. Counts and hard-constraint results still belong in an automated
audit; they should not consume the human reading exercise.

Output-contract failures, unsupported necessity claims, and incorrect attribution
remain engineering findings. They do not become evidence of reader dissatisfaction,
nor does a literary tie retroactively validate an invalid report. In particular,
the earlier review tests asked for supportable accounts of changes and trade-offs,
not merely a preference winner.

The provisional 48-call reviewer and 24-call writer proposals remain unprepared and
unauthorized. Before either is frozen, revise its editorial scoring to distinguish
observable content change from material reader harm, permit supported ties, and
avoid treating agreement with the assistant's preferred wording as ground truth.
Keep clear technical controls for source binding, report delivery, no-op invariance,
and genuine obligation failures. New story contexts will be more informative for
human quality work than another packet of small changes to this ending.

## Future division of review work

**Humans judge the reading experience.** Ask about overall feel and emotional impact,
basic story flow, character motivation and credibility, and anything that breaks
the story or pulls the reader out. Invite examples where they help, without requiring
line-by-line evidence collection or repeated counts. A tie and no major concerns
are valid responses.

**Machines prepare mechanics and recorded outcomes.** Before requesting human
review, prepare exact versions, diffs, repetition counts, repaired versus outstanding
requests, completion checks, and existing critic results with their validity status.
Use deterministic code for exact text operations. Existing records should not require
new model calls or human reconstruction.

**Semantic checks need supported interpretations.** A machine can locate candidate
evidence for a decision, but a phrase match is not by itself proof of intention.
Supply relevant surrounding text, distinguish the evidence from the interpretation,
and surface genuine ambiguity or contradictions. Finding a repeated phrase is
mechanical; deciding whether it is accidental duplication or effective recurrence
is an interpretation. Do not replace the previous overloaded critic with an
unqualified keyword gate.

**Keep the first reading free of verdicts.** Prepare the machine appendix in advance,
but keep its judgments out of the initial literary reading. After the reader records
an impression, show only material findings and unresolved exceptions for optional
confirmation. This preserves useful blinding without making the reader reconstruct
the mechanical audit.

**Reduce repeated reading.** For informal diagnostic review, ordinarily use one
complete version per story or one meaningfully different A/B pair per reader and
story family. Keep near-identical variants in the machine diagnostic set. When many
variants matter, distribute them across readers or sessions and record familiarity;
do not imply that repeated versions are independent stories. Formal benchmark
coverage remains governed by its frozen plan.

Use the [short human review template](human-story-review-template-2026-10-20.md).
This changes evaluation preparation and presentation, not production prompts, graph
routing, the mandatory Blueprint checkpoint, accepted whole-story rubric weights,
or benchmark thresholds. No unprovided numeric scores are inferred from this review.
