## How strongly does this read as an AI-generated story?

`The Alchemist's Latitude` strongly resembles an AI-generated story. In the very first sentence, our writer uses a very common scene description pattern seen in LLM-generated stories. This pattern often uses **smell**, **taste** or **scent** followed by sentence modifiers. 

`The cabin of the Aethelgard smelled of brine, old parchment, and the sharp, metallic tang of a man who had forgotten to sleep.`  

Our writer repeats many known AI tics. Contrastive framing appears at least 25 times in the story.

>Note! Below are samples, not the full list of AI tics found in The Alchemist's Latitude. Some of the examples display more than one tic in a sentence.

**Contrastive framing**:

- Scene 1: "The world **is not a fixed sphere**, **but a series of overlapping possibilities**."
- Scene 1: "**I deal in dead reckoning and deep-sea navigation**, **not the tangents** of a man who was laughed out of the Royal Court."

- Scene 1: "The history they wrote of me—the 'error' that cost me my standing—**was not a mistake**, **but a glimpse of a larger truth**."

## Opinion

>Note! The following opinion is for The Alchemist's Latitude as it was outlined in Open Hollywood's pre-production and later created in production after blind human approval.

`The Alchemist's Latitude` starts with a great premise but never materializes into a good story. Throughout the story, we only get a glimpse of what this journey could have been. Our main character, Elias Thorne, is a disgraced Royal Cartographer, but we never learn what actually happened to him. Why is he disgraced? Mira says he was "...laughed out of the Royal Court." But why?

Aside from a few funny bits involving Mira's crew forgetting who they are, the fact that the journey alters humans never actually changes the storyline in a meaningful way. It never acts as a disruptor or a challenge. The journey goes on as scheduled, and Mira's crew is kind of forgotten. Why altering a route on the map didn't cause Mira and Elias to forget anything, as it did her crew, is another question and a missed chance to create a story twist.

Our Story Blueprint (in the section about the "World") says: 
`...altering a route on these maps rewrites the history and identity of everyone aboard the vessel...` 

Regardless of the Story Blueprint, our writer could've made an exception for the two main characters, but then at least spiced up the journey. Perhaps one alteration doesn't cause a complete loss, but a sort of buzz. But then the second one enhances that buzz and creates some interesting subplots.

The way it is written, The Alchemist's Latitude is a story of missed opportunities.
