## How strongly does this read as an AI-generated story?

The Enchanted Academy reads strongly as an AI-generated story. In the very first sentence, our writer uses a very common scene description pattern seen in LLM-generated stories. This pattern often uses **smell**, **taste** or **scent** followed by sentence modifiers. 

`The air in the lecture hall tasted of ozone and old parchment, a sterile combination that always made Elara’s throat tighten.`

Our writer often uses many of the common AI tics. We can see contrastive framing at least 25 times. In addition, our writer often summarizes scenes and writes in a way that resembles scripts for documentary movies.

>Note! Below are samples, not the full list of AI tics found in The Enchanted Academy.

**Contrastive framing**:

- Scene 1: "Magic **is not a suggestion**; **it is a law**."
- Scene 1: "A single misplaced syllable **is not a mistake**—**it is a failure of will**."
- Scene 1: "**Lyra wasn't looking** at the hovering crystal; **she was looking** directly into Elara’s eyes."
- Scene 2: "His eyes were wide and restless, darting across Elara’s face with an intensity that **was not predatory**, **but desperately curious**."
- Scene 2: "**You aren't just slipping**, Elara. **You are echoing**."

**Triplet framing**:

- Scene 3: "As she opened the pages, the handwriting mirrored her own—**sharp**, **hurried**, and **filled with a desperate urgency**."

**Quirks and questionable decisions**:

- Scene 1: "Elara’s fingers trembled as she reached for the crystal focus on her desk. **Her goal was simple: a basic kinetic lift, a standard demonstration of the Academy's approved arts.** But as she began the incantation, the pressure in the room seemed to thicken. She could feel the gaze of the faculty, the invisible threads of surveillance weaving through the air, searching for any sign of instability."
>Comment! Why don't we write what she's doing and let readers reach the conclusion? We can write something like this: "She performed a basic kinetic lift, her fingers controlling the shards like a puppet master." This sentence fits in the context, tells readers what she's doing, and tells readers that she's good at what she's doing.

- Scene 1: "Elara breathed in, **the scent of ozone sharpening**. She whispered the final word of the spell, but her voice cracked. The crystal **didn't lift; instead,** it shuddered and shattered, sending a spray of iridescent shards across the marble tabletop."
>Comment! Let's write what moves the story forward. Example: "Elara breathed in. She whispered the final word of the spell, but her voice cracked. The crystal shuddered and shattered, sending a spray of iridescent shards across the marble tabletop."

- Scene 1: "Elara shrank back into her seat, the victory of the lesson vanishing. **She was no longer just a timid apprentice; she was a secret, and Lyra had just found the key.**"
>Comment! The last sentence feels like a cheesy conclusion/summary of what we already know.

- Scene 2: "**He didn't wait for her answer.** With a sudden, fluid motion, he retreated back into the shadows of the obsidian pillar, leaving Elara alone in the purple twilight, the scent of ozone still lingering in the air like a promise or a threat."
>Comment! The second sentence already gives a clue that he didn't wait for her answer. If we drop the first sentence, we achieve the same goal with fewer words.

- Scene 3: "Elara looked back at the chronicle, then up at the oppressive darkness of the archives. For the first time in her life, the weight of the Academy's laws felt fragile. **She wasn't a glitch in a perfect system; she was the evidence that the system was a lie.** She closed the book and gripped it tightly against her chest."
>Comment! I believe the sentence in bold should be dropped. It is unnecessary. When you read the rest of the text without that sentence, you realize you don't miss anything.

- Scene 4: "As she approached the heavy, obsidian doors of the Headmaster’s sanctum, Lyra paused. She adjusted the cuff of her sleeve, her mind racing. **For days, she had watched Elara—the clumsy, stuttering girl who should have been a footnote in the academy’s history. Lyra had seen the flicker in the lecture hall, the impossible regression of the crystal. It was a power that defied the Ancient Laws, a gift that Lyra, for all her study and rigorous discipline, could only ever hope to simulate. The envy was a cold, hard knot in her stomach, a bitterness that tasted of copper and ozone.**"
>Comment! We already know a lot of the things highlighted in bold. We know that Lyra witnessed Elara manipulating time. But if we want to show that she's really jealous, then we should show that. Lyra could've confronted Elara and threatened to expose her secret to Headmaster Thorne. That would show jealousy. As it stands, we're narrating things like in a documentary movie.

- Scene 4: "Lyra felt a surge of anticipation. **This was the moment—the bridge between being a mere tool of the state and becoming an indispensable instrument of the Inquisition.** "It is Elara. I witnessed her during the kinetic lift lesson. She did not merely fail the incantation; she rewound it. A three-second temporal stutter, executed with a fluidity that suggests innate capability rather than accidental leakage.""
>Comment! If we drop the sentence in bold and read the rest, we don't lose anything.

- Scene 4: "Lyra stiffened. The validation she craved felt hollow. **He wasn't praising her skill; he was acknowledging the arrival of a tool he already intended to use. The envy in her chest flared, hotter now. She wanted to be the one he relied upon, not the one who merely pointed the way to another's power.**"
>Comment! We've read what the Headmaster said; our writer doesn't need to summarize it for us. Lyra coming to tell the Headmaster what she had witnessed is enough for us to know what she wants. If we drop all three sentences in bold and continue reading the story, we see that we don't lose anything.

- Scene 4: "Lyra stepped aside to let him pass, her expression a mask of disciplined obedience. But as Thorne swept out of the room, his robes billowing behind him, Lyra looked down at the shimmering shard on the desk. **The hunt had begun, and while Thorne sought a key, Lyra realized she was still just the lock-keeper.**"
>Comment! Can Lyra offer to help in the search for Elara and get dismissed? That would've been a much better way for us to learn how she feels instead of being told this way. I would drop the last sentence.

- Scene 5: "Running is such a tedious exertion, Elara. It lacks the precision I expect from my students."
>Comment! In my opinion, this is a very strange line of dialogue.

## Opinion

>Note! The following opinion is for The Enchanted Academy as it was outlined in Open Hollywood's pre-production and later created in production after blind human approval.

`The Enchanted Academy` starts well. Our protagonist, Elara, acquires the ability to shift time: chronomancy. We also see Lyra, Elara's rival, witnessing Elara learning to shift time. Then we get to Kaelen.

Our writer missed a big opportunity that could've enriched our story. Kaelen could've learned about Elara's new ability by overhearing Lyra's conversation with Elara, where Lyra could've threatened to expose Elara's secret. After all, Lyra's Elara's rival. Then, Kaelen could have offered his help to Elara. This would've played nicely with Scene 4, where Lyra finally tells Headmaster Thorne about Elara's ability to manipulate time.

Headmaster Thorne is a good villain. In Scene 5, when Elara mentions that her parents would've hated him, Thorne admits to killing them. This raises the stakes and pushes our protagonist towards her goals.

`The Enchanted Academy` is a decent story, but I feel that, had our writer explored Elara's rivalry with Lyra a bit more, we might have ended up with a really good story.
