## How strongly does this read as an AI-generated story?

`Space Detective` reads strongly as an AI-generated story. In the very first sentence, our writer uses a very common scene description pattern seen in LLM-generated stories. This pattern often uses **smell**, **taste** or **scent** followed by sentence modifiers. 

`The Hub smelled of recycled ozone and desperation.`

Our writer goes on and repeats many well-known AI tics that usually appear in LLM-generated text. Contrastive framing features more than 10 times in the story.  

>Note! Below are samples, not the full list of AI tics found in Space Detective. Some of the examples display more than one tic in a sentence.

**Contrastive framing**:

- Scene 1: "**She didn't start with the official requests**; **she knew how the corporate game worked.**"
- Scene 1: "**This wasn't** a series of accidents or runaway settlers. **It was** a harvest."
- Scene 2: "**Vance didn't sit**. **She remained standing**, her silhouette jagged against the sterile perfection of the room."
- Scene 2: "**These aren't clerical errors**. **These are scrubs**."
- Scene 2: "**Kane wasn't just covering up a crime**; **he was the architect of the system**."

**Triplet framing**:

- Scene 1: "She began pulling the census data for the lower sectors—**the poor**, **the transients**, **the people the company viewed as disposable**."
- Scene 3: "There were **no signs of a struggle**, **no blood**, **no desperate claw marks** in the rock."
- Scene 3: "It wasn't the jagged edge of ore or the rusted pipe of a drainage line. **It was smooth**. **Cold**. **Synthetic**."
- Scene 5: "They had been stripped of everything that made them human—**limbs**, **skin**, **organs**—until only the neural tissue remained."

**Sensory language in scene descriptions**:

>Note! In the samples below, the sensory words are highlighted in italics, while the modifiers are in bold.

- Scene 2: "The air grew thinner, the *smell* of **recycled ozone** sharpening into something **clinical** and **cold**."
- Scene 4: "The air here was different—colder, *smelling* of **sterile ozone** and **something faintly organic, like wet earth and old copper**."
- Scene 6: "Vance stood in the darkness, breathing in the *smell* of **ozone** and **ozone-burnt circuitry**."

**Quirks and questionable decisions**:

- Scene 2: "Vance backed away from the desk, her gaze never leaving his. **She had come for answers, and she had found them. She couldn't trust the badge in her pocket, the air she breathed, or the man smiling at her from behind the obsidian. She was alone in the spire, and the machine was hungry**." 
>Comment! We already know what Elara Vance is doing, and this feels like some sort of summary.

- Scene 3: "She adjusted the seal of her mask, her eyes scanning the desolate landscape. **To the corporate overseers, this was a site of production**; to Vance, it looked like a mass grave waiting for a name."
>Comment! If there are corporate men at this location, let's show this instead of telling us how they see it. What do they do? How do they act? If they are not here, I feel this sentence is obsolete.

- Scene 3: "She knelt, her gloved fingers sifting through the metallic grit. The ground here felt wrong—too level, too deliberate. She began to clear away the sulfurous dust, her movements rhythmic and obsessive. **She had spent a lifetime searching for things that were meant to stay lost, and she knew that the truth rarely sat on the surface. It preferred the dark.**"
>Comment! We already know Elara is a detective and is searching for the truth. So that part feels like a summary. But then, our writer goes on to tell us that truth preferred the dark, and it feels like a comment.

- Scene 5: "Vance gripped the drive in her hand, her back to the vats of screaming minds. She was trapped, the exit blocked, the air thick with the scent of a thousand deaths, but for the first time in years, the ghosts were silent. **She had the truth, and she would burn the world down to make sure it was heard**."
>Comment! The text in bold is telling us things that we already know.

- Scene 6: "Vance stood in the darkness, breathing in the smell of **ozone** and **ozone-burnt circuitry**."
>Comment! Using ozone twice as a modifier.

We are reminded twice that Administrator Kane wears a charcoal suit after we established that in Scene 2. Although our scenes happen at different times, I'm of the opinion that once we establish what characters wear, we don't need to remind the readers of the colors or patterns of our characters' clothing unless they change. The charcoal suit can simply be a "suit" in all other instances.

- Scene 5: "At the entrance of the sanctum stood Administrator Kane. He looked out of place in the organic gloom, **his charcoal silk suit** pristine, his expression one of mild, disappointed boredom."
- Scene 6: "Administrator Kane stepped forward, **his charcoal silk suit** a sharp, sterile contrast to the organic gore of the vault."

## Opinion

>Note! The following opinion is for Space Detective as it was outlined in Open Hollywood's pre-production and later created in production after blind human approval.

`Space Detective` uses an interesting premise but, unfortunately, turns it into a sterile sci-fi story. Our detective, Elara Vance, pretty quickly finds out that people are vanishing from the colony. When Administrator Kane gives her a warning about continuing the investigation, Elara simply ignores him and goes on.

Until the very end, Elara never gets in trouble for her choice. There's no attempt at a twist. For example, Elara could've given up after Kane threatened her, but then she could've met somebody or experienced something to change her mind.

Then we meet Unit 7-Echo. Unit 7-Echo appears to know who Elara Vance is even though this is the first time they meet. He ends his dialogue with `Vance`, Elara's last name, but speaks as if he has never met her before.

`Was...* the voice replied, a glitchy shudder running through the audio. *I was... Sector 4. I was... a father. I was... a name. Now I am... processing... load-bearing... neural... architecture. We are the... hardware... Vance.*`

When somebody calls you by your name and you believe you've never met that person, or a semi-sentient hybrid in this case, a human being's reaction would most likely be:
`Where do we know each other from?` Or some other form of that question.

Elara never identified herself when meeting 7-Echo, but okay, maybe 7-Echo saw her detective badge. What if 7-Echo and Elara actually did meet before? Because 7-Echo is no longer human, it is understandable that Elara might not recognize him. That could've been an interesting thing to explore in the story, but our writer failed to turn this encounter into a subplot.

Overall, I feel Space Detective uses too much fluff and not enough character development to turn the premise into a good story.
