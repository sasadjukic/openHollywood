## How strongly does this read as an AI-generated story?

`Unreleased` reads strongly as an AI-generated story. In the very first sentence, our writer uses a very common scene description pattern seen in LLM-generated stories. This pattern often uses **smell**, **taste** or **scent** followed by sentence modifiers.

`The Author's Study smelled of old parchment, stale tobacco, and the oppressive weight of a thousand bound volumes.`

In the rest of the story, our writer uses other common AI tics, mostly relying on contrastive framing. Contrastive framing appears 20 times in the story.

## Opinion

>Note! The following opinion is for Unreleased as it was outlined in Open Hollywood's pre-production and later created in production after blind human approval. This is not my attempt to rewrite the story from scratch.

`Unreleased` is a decent story with good characters. What I believe would make this story even better is further development of our antagonist, Beatrice. Beatrice never issues any kind of threat to Clara. The threat could've been veiled, but it should have made clear to us what the stakes are if Clara goes on with the investigation. Instead, Beatrice mildly warns Clara.

Clara's investigation never really stalls or hits a major bump. She never really struggles with anything. 

Our writer uses too many summaries and too much story filler. If these were trimmed, there would be enough space (in terms of word count and story length) to create a good subplot that would've made `Unreleased` a really good story.
