## Opinion
>Note! The following opinion is for The Butter Incident as it was outlined in Open Hollywood's pre-production and later created in production after blind human approval.

`The Butter Incident` starts well, building our character through a series of clumsy mistakes. During the first couple of scenes, I feel there's a lack of dialogue to break up the long baking routine and offer more info about the food critic, to build her up before we even meet her. What newspaper or online media company is she writing for? What's her taste? Our story artifacts even mention that Stella had panned Julian's previous attempts at 'fusion' cooking in a scathing review three years prior. All of that could've been done with some odd jokes or him just commenting on his previous experience with Stella or food critics in general.

While the ending is fine, the way we reached it is a bit suspicious. Stella Thorne was introduced as the food critic in the first scene with this sentence:

`The mere thought of the critic—her palate like a razor, her reviews like executioner's axes—sent a jolt through Julian's wiry frame.` 

Food critics do not go into restaurant kitchens. They always sit at the best place in the restaurant and are served by the best servers. Our writer missed an opportunity to have Julian personally bring his bread to her while looking messed up from baking. Or perhaps, due to mistakes while baking, Julian didn't want to take his bread for tasting at all. After all, he must have thought Stella would've ripped him apart with her critique, but Stella could've kind of forced him anyway. Then their conversation, together with her verdict, could've made an even greater impact.

Overall, `The Butter Incident` is a decent story that could've been way spicier.
