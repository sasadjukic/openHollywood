## How strongly does this read as an AI-generated story?

`Perfect Silence` reads strongly as an AI-generated story. In the very first sentence, our writer uses a very common scene description pattern seen in LLM-generated stories. This pattern often uses **smell**, **taste** or **scent** followed by sentence modifiers. 

`The Sanctuary smelled of ozone and eucalyptus, a scent profile meticulously chosen for its ability to signal cognitive reset.` 

`Perfect Silence` repeats the most common AI writing tics throughout the story. Contrastive framing appears at least 15 times in the story.

>Note! Below are samples, not the full list of AI tics found in Perfect Silence. Some of the examples display more than one tic in a sentence. 

**Contrastive framing**:

- Scene 1: "**He didn't just want** silence; **he wanted** the curated silence—a specific, engineered void where the frequency of the machine perfectly canceled out the distant hum of the city."
- Scene 1: "**He was not merely a man** in a room; **he was the administrator** of a closed system."
- Scene 1: "**It was no longer a sanctuary**; **it was a vacuum**, and he was suffocating in it."
- Scene 1: "**He was no longer breathing** the ozone; **he was inhaling** the scent of his own inadequacy."
- Scene 2: "She held the Tupperware container forward, **not as a gift**, **but as a barrier**."

**Triplet framing**:

- Scene 2: "The voice was a sonic assault—**loquacious**, **bright**, and **entirely too loud** for a residential corridor. Arthur froze."
- Scene 2: "*Exit strategy Alpha. The 'Mat Maneuver.' **Fast**. **Efficient**. **Clean**. Get inside. Lock the door. Reset the decibels."
- Scene 5: "He was prepared **for Arthur to be rigid**, **for Arthur to be failing**, **for Arthur to be desperate**."
- Scene 5: "He saw **the oatmeal linen**, **the calculated posture**, **the smug**, pseudo-zen mask."

**Repeated words or phrases in similar contexts**:

Our writer reminds us 6 times what Barbara is wearing. Some cases are fine, especially the one from scene 2 because it establishes her outfit for the first time, but I believe two cases from scenes 4 and 5 are redundant.
- Scene 2: "**She wore a floral housecoat** that defied any known color theory and carried a translucent plastic Tupperware container with a lid that sat slightly askew."
- Scene 4: "He was not the administrator of a system; he was a man on his knees in a room full of broken ceramics, being pitied **by a woman in a floral housecoat**."
- Scene 5: "**She was still wearing the floral housecoat**, her presence a vivid, chaotic smudge against the sterile gray of the Sanctuary."

**Quirks and questionable decisions**:

- Scene 4: "He looked around his apartment. The white walls now looked like the interior of a padded cell. The air purifier was just a humming plastic box. The ergonomic chair was a joke. The curated silence was a lie. **He was not the administrator of a system; he was a man on his knees in a room full of broken ceramics, being pitied by a woman in a floral housecoat.**"
>Comment! I believe that last sentence is not telling us anything new, so it feels like a summary.

>Comment! When showing that something is expensive, LLMs like to make comparisons, especially comparing an item to a character's car. There are two such examples in `Perfect Silence`.
- Scene 4: "On that pedestal sat the 'Void-Form No. 4,' a sculpture of matte-black ceramic **that cost more than Arthur's first car**."
- Scene 5: "He wore a linen suit in a shade of 'transcendental oatmeal' **that likely cost more than Arthur's monthly mortgage**."

In `Perfect Silence`, our writer writes Barbara's full name in scene 5, although she's been part of the story since scene 2:
- Scene 5: "**Barbara Plum** hovered over him, her face a map of maternal pity."

## Opinion

>Note! The following opinion is for Perfect Silence as it was outlined in Open Hollywood's pre-production and later created in production after blind human approval. 

`Perfect Silence` starts well. Our characters are interesting and weird in a good way. But somewhere along the way, our writer loses it. The character development stalls, and their initial depth is all we get. Our dialogue starts out funny, but wears out by the time the story ends.

Because Barbara is a spontaneous and social person, we may assume she knows other neighbors. Maybe she met Marcus and chatted with him; maybe she had coffee with him once. Because Marcus is Arthur's rival, Barbara's opinion of Marcus could have provoked reactions from Arthur (positive or negative). This could've opened up our story a bit, given it more depth, and explored Arthur's rivalry with Marcus.

Instead of building on a good start, our writer begins to tell us what we will experience and draws conclusions about what we have already experienced. The following sentence from scene 5 tells us what Barbara is wearing:

`She was still wearing the floral housecoat...`

We know what Barbara is wearing. She's been part of the story since scene 2 and never left. She couldn't have changed clothes without us knowing that. Aside from that, we've been told at least five times what she was wearing.

I feel `Perfect Silence` could've been much better, especially considering its good start.
