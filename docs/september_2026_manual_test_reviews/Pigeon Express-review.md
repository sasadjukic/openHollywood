## Opinion

>Note! The following opinion is for Pigeon Express as it was outlined in Open Hollywood's pre-production and later created in production after blind human approval.

`Pigeon Express` starts with a mystery and with us learning about the city's grid, which is a decent start. But the characters' actions and dialogue never truly tell us who Elias and Mara are or the reasons behind their actions.

To find out about Elias and Mara, we need to read the story artifacts, and from the following passage in scene 3, we must assume they know each other:

`"You're late," he grunted, his voice like grinding gravel. "And you're breathing too loud."`

Elias then agrees to help Mara. Why? What drives him to help her? The story artifacts say that Elias is "a disgraced former intelligence officer". Did he help put an innocent person in jail? Does he regret working for the state and now want to help the resistance?

Mara is in the resistance movement. Why? Did she witness the state doing something horrible to a person she knew? We never learn anything about her background.

We also never learn anything about the resistance. What do they fight for? How long have they been fighting?  

Characters, and people in real life, never wake up with a bag full of beliefs. It is learning experiences, the environment, the actions of others, etc., that form their opinions and beliefs.

The action that follows in scenes 4 and 5 feels empty because we don't know why Elias and Mara are doing all this. Sure, we see that they want a better world, but to make all that believable, and for us to actually care, we need to connect emotionally with our protagonists. And we never do.

The story appears to be fluffed with filler and unnecessary conclusions. It tells us what our characters are not doing or things that our characters are not aware of.

Pigeon Express has a lot of problems, but to try to sum it up: our writer wrote a linear, sterile story that ended with a big MEH, despite Elias sacrificing his life.
