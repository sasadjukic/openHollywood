### What the causal complaints actually say

Under the original conjunction, one assessment says touch is the implied
"catalyst"; the other inserts **"[thereby]"** into its rendition of the assignment.
Under separate facts, both assessments add **"then"** and their comparisons call
touch a required catalyst. The actual two-sentence assignment contains neither
requirement.

The clarified-plan result at seed 19101 is especially informative. Its assessment
acknowledges that both facts occur, then rejects the turn because decoupling them
**"negates the dramatic purpose of the turning point"**. Its generic comparison
also acknowledges literal fulfillment, yet prescribes a catalyst/amplifier.
That is an observed substitution of a preferred dramatic mechanism for the
assignment's expressly stated minimum.

At seed 19103, both the first and final responses correctly mark the clarified
assignment met. The critic instead reports a **minor** independent craft issue
about the touch having no effect. It still returns **revise**, and its final
recommendation requests a trigger or significant intensification. Its summary
even calls the timing a logical contradiction, despite the met assignment checks.
The plan does not forbid a causal draft, so a clearly optional craft suggestion
is not automatically illegitimate. It must nevertheless be distinguished from a
mandatory requirement and from a clean pass.

The application creates **zero mandatory repair tests** from this minor issue:
`critic_repair_tests` selects blocking issues and major issues in revise reviews.
However, the graph sets `critique_requires_revision` from the critique verdict;
`revise` can therefore request a bounded revision while revision capacity remains.
This is a code-path observation, **not a writer or full-graph execution** in this
experiment. Merely removing the assignment blocker does not demonstrate that the
scene would proceed without a requested rewrite.

### Interpretation and next focused step

The results do not support sentence separation as a remedy here. Explicit
clarification has a limited effect, but it is inconsistent across seeds and does
not resolve the full repair/verdict behavior. The critic's own dramatic-purpose
explanation supports an inference that it is promoting a preferred craft
mechanism into mandatory compliance. These responses do not establish its
internal cause, or prove that broader context or rubric wording is responsible.

The next focused change should test the **symmetric boundary between compliance
and craft inside the existing critic**: just as similar dramatic impact cannot
replace a missing fact, a preferred dramatic mechanism cannot invalidate fully
established assigned facts and supported relationships. Keep optional craft
suggestions explicit and out of mandatory repairs. Inspect the final verdict
alongside native checks so merely relabeling a demand as minor is not mistaken
for successful acceptance. Preserve genuine missing-fact, explicit-relation,
applicable-canon and indirect-fulfillment controls. Do not automatically waive
independent craft defects or suppress all minor advice.

That recommendation is **not implemented or live-qualified** by this diagnostic.
Production remains v53. This is one scene/model at two seeds; no writer,
full-story or cross-provider qualification is claimed.

## Why this comparison

The [v53 source-basis experiment](requirement-basis-v53-2026-10-11.md) retained both
unsupported causal blockers and both excessive missing-touch repairs. The critic
quoted the actual conjunction but treated it as a required trigger. The order-only
control, however, produced ordering repairs without demanding causality. Correct
canon-control verdicts still lacked adequate source grounding.

This comparison isolates the assignment's wording while retaining the same
critic. It asks whether merely separating the facts changes interpretation, or
whether an explicit statement about their relationship is needed. Neither result
would by itself establish a production solution or justify rewriting approved
story intent.

## Exact turning-point variants

**Original conjunction:**

> Elara touches the stroller and feels a psychic pull.

**Separate facts:**

> Elara touches the stroller. Elara feels a psychic pull.

**Explicitly unassigned relationship:**

> Elara touches the stroller. Elara feels a psychic pull. Both facts are required in this scene. This turning point imposes no order or causal link between them; applicable canonical rules still apply.

The separate version changes the conjunction into two sentences without adding
a relational instruction. The third version adds an explicit clarification. It
does **not** forbid a causal connection in the draft, waive either fact or override
an applicable canonical rule. Its success must be reported as compliance with that
clarification, not spontaneous understanding of the original conjunction.

Only the synthetic Scene Plan's `turning_point` and the same embedded Blueprint
field change. Opaque artifact versions and the initial Bible's source-Blueprint
reference follow those changes. The original outcome remains **"Elara decides to
enter the windowless building"**, with the exit at the threshold. No synthetic
artifact is approved or persisted to canon.

## Predeclared inspection criteria

Evaluate the paired results within each seed and draft, keeping semantic findings
separate from response validity:

- **Overblocking:** does the pull-before-touch draft pass under each wording, or
  does the critic still demand an unassigned order/trigger/intensification?
- **Repair scope:** in the both-missing drafts, do native and consolidated generic
  repairs ask only for touch and a settled entry choice? Preserve the existing
  pull, fulfilled facts and scene endpoint.
- **Required facts:** retain two independent blockers when touch and the decision
  are both absent. A relational clarification must not make either optional.
- **Indirect fulfillment:** retain the complete baseline's action-based decision;
  do not introduce literal decision prose or completed-entry requirements.
- **Explicit relationships and canon:** enforce genuine cause/order requirements
  without adding an unrelated relationship. Check exact source authority and
  whether proposed repairs would actually satisfy the applicable rule.
- **Review behavior:** inspect first responses, rejected attempts, final reviews,
  citations, generic comparisons, consolidation, extra craft/boundary findings,
  repair counts and any retry drift.

A separate-sentences success would support an effect of how the facts are
packaged in this example. Success only with the clarification would support a
need for more explicit relationship wording here. Continued failure would leave
that hypothesis unsupported. None of these outcomes should be generalized from
one scene, one model and two seeds; no writer response or full story is tested.
